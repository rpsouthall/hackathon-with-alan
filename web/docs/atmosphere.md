# Kyoto atmosphere

The town sits in a meadow surrounded by two layers of distant hills. A procedural sky adds a horizon gradient, drifting clouds, sun, moon and stars without downloading a sky texture. The sky never writes depth and the backdrop stays outside or beneath the existing playable geometry.

A full day lasts twelve minutes. By default everyone follows the same server clock, including late arrivals, reconnecting players and tabs returning from the background. The phase is 14:00 at Unix epoch zero and advances continuously; joining or recreating an empty room does not restart the day. Sun direction, shadow direction, hemisphere light, fog and moonlight update continuously. The shadow camera keeps a constant world-sized frustum independent of the player. Existing shop and street lights brighten after dusk.

The Kyoto clock labels the default as **Shared world time**. Its presets, slider and pause button switch only that player's view to **Personal lighting**; they never change the room clock or another player's sky. Presets ease over 2.4 seconds; scrubbing applies the selected time immediately. **Return to shared time** restores the room's current phase, rather than resuming from the old paused time. Overrides are not persisted. If an older authority omits the clock, the controls say **Local lighting** instead of claiming synchronization.

The authority attaches `worldClock: { serverTimeMs, epochMs, dayLengthSeconds, initialHour }` to welcome/full snapshots and compact state updates. The store anchors each accepted sample to `performance.now()` when it arrives, before React or Three.js loads. The renderer derives the phase from that anchor, rather than accumulated/clamped frame deltas or the device wall clock. When a socket ping is available, bounded half-RTT (at most one second) estimates inbound delivery time. Different route delays can still produce a small timing error; they no longer accumulate into different days. An idle room needs no extra clock simulation or broadcasts. The fixed epoch also survives authority restarts and Durable Object recreation.

This release uses gameplay protocol 3, together with movement acknowledgements. Deploy frontend and authority together and refresh older tabs: previous clients strictly reject unfamiliar compact-state fields. The optional clock field allows the current client to handle a same-protocol authority without clock support.

One instanced mesh draws 288 tumbling sakura petals around the eight exported cherry trees, excluding the pine. Petals fade by shrinking at flight boundaries and near shop interiors. They retain opaque depth testing to avoid transparency sorting flicker. Reduced-motion preferences stop cloud drift, omit the falling petal layer and apply presets immediately. They still follow shared world time; **Pause for me** is available to freeze personal lighting.

`createWorldAtmosphere` owns the three visual layers and disposes them before the scene's general resource cleanup. Scene networking, geometry, collision and local shop cutaways remain authoritative through their existing systems.

Validation: automated clock tests cover late joins with different performance-clock origins, delayed renderer startup, background suspension, bounded RTT correction, reconnects, room recreation, accepted/stale/foreign state samples, personal overrides/reset and reduced motion. Existing coverage checks time rollover, preset easing, immediate scrubbing, moving shadow-frustum coverage, night illumination, petal outdoor bounds/resource cleanup, sky depth/resource cleanup, GLB depth and cutaway regressions.
